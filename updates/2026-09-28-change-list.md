### 4. Anthropic's D.C. Circuit challenge to the § 4713 (FASCSA) designation — Section G.2 — **petition denied**
- Case: *Anthropic PBC v. U.S. Department of War*, **Nos. 26-1049 and 26-1162 (D.C. Cir.)**. Filed Mar 9, 2026 (same day as the district complaint, not merely "anticipated"); stay denied Apr 8; argued May 19; supplemental briefs June; **decided September 25, 2026, 2–1**, Katsas (writing) and Rao in majority, Henderson dissenting. Held the FASCSA exclusion lawful: "manipulate" reaches a contractor that "disable[s] Claude from performing lawful actions requested by the Department"; the First Amendment claim failed because the exclusion rested on "refusal to assent to a contract term that the Department deemed essential." Dissent: "manipulation" requires deceptive or covert interference, not "a contractor's honest and upfront enforcement of restrictions." Anthropic: "considering all options, including further review."
- Sources: Justia opinion page (26-1049, 2026-09-25; blocked at fetch), Defense One 9/25, Nextgov 9/26, Reason/Volokh 9/25, RedState 9/25, Jones Walker (stay denial), A&O Shearman chronology. **Opinion not read** (Justia/CourtListener blocked; cadc.uscourts.gov listing 404).
- Rating: MED-HIGH (case number, date, panel and holding concur across several sources; opinion text not opened). Retrieval path: cadc.uscourts.gov opinions, Sept 25, 2026, No. 26-1049.
- Tracker text to change: row title "Anthropic's anticipated D.C. Circuit challenge" and "No D.C. Circuit petition was located through Sept. 5" are both outdated. Section G row for the Feb 27 directive should note that the two statutory bases now have opposite outcomes.

## B. New candidate entries (in scope under the tracker's definition)

### 19. California Executive Order **N-9-26** (Sept 18, 2026) — new row for Section G-equivalent state executive action, or a note under A/C
- Directs GovOps (with Cal OES) to convene national experts and submit recommendations by **Nov 16, 2026** on at least four amendments to state law: (1) require all large frontier developers to embed designated IVOs **onsite** for periodic audits and evaluations; (2) independent verification of the frameworks, transparency reports and risk assessments frontier companies must file; (3) a **"kill switch" for frontier models, with the efficacy of the switch verified on an ongoing basis**; (4) update the definition of critical safety incident to include **loss-of-control incidents**. Accelerates SB 813 (IVO application requirements by May 1, 2027) and AB 1405 (online auditor registration by Dec 1, 2027). Experts named Sept 23: Jason Goldman, Gillian Hadfield, Alondra Nelson, Rob Reich.
- Sources: gov.ca.gov releases 9/18 and 9/23 (read); GovTech Insider; ppc.land (deadline detail). **Order text not read.**
- Rating: MED-HIGH (official press releases quote the directives; EO text not opened). Retrieval: gov.ca.gov executive orders, N-9-26.
- Why in scope: directly targets large frontier developers and amends the SB 53 / SB 813 / AB 1405 regime.

### 20. Illinois Executive Order **2026-07** (Sept 22, 2026) — Illinois AI Cabinet
- Establishes an AI Cabinet (DoIT, IEMA-OHS, IDFPR, ICC, ISP, IDPH, IEPA plus outside experts; appointments within 30 days; sunset Dec 31, 2027) to develop policies to prepare for and respond to AI incidents, protect public assets, and evaluate further regulation including conditioning data-center incentives on safety standards. The order cites the AI Safety Measures Act (SB 315) and its audit and 72-hour incident-reporting duties.
- Sources: illinois.gov EO 2026-07 (read); Governor's release 9/22. Rating: HIGH (order text read online).

### 22. **Artificial Intelligence Risk Management and Security Act of 2026** — Warner, Schatz, Kim — Section D candidate
- Introduced **Sept 24, 2026** (senators spoke on the floor the same day; MLex reports a unanimous-consent attempt was planned). Text (sponsor PDF, read in part): establishes an **AI Safety Board** in Commerce (NIST, CISA, NSA, Treasury, outside experts) within 90 days; "frontier artificial intelligence model" = a model "that exhibits or could be modified to exhibit high levels of performance at tasks that pose a serious risk to national security, national economic security, or public health or safety" (capability-based, **no compute figure**); developers must give the Board access **≥45 days before public release** including weights, configuration files, runtimes and libraries; Model Safety Plans naming a responsible corporate officer; incident reporting **30 days / 72 hours** if imminent; national incident database; secure federal testing environments; civil penalties **up to $250,000 per violation per day**.
- **S. number not located** (GovInfo BILLSTATUS probed S. 5535–5556; Warner's PDF is the "S. ll" pre-introduction print). Retrieval: congress.gov search "Artificial Intelligence Risk Management and Security Act".
- Rating: MED-HIGH on provisions (sponsor bill text read), LOW on number until located. Relationship: successor to Warner's S. 5061 (NSA testing) — worth a "succession" edge on the Map.
